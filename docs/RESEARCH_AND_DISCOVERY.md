# Sprints 1–2 discovery and problem analysis

## Initial concept
FinSight was proposed as a central way to find, filter and understand information scattered across financial news, online discussions, company announcements and stock-market data. It is meant to support independent investor research, not execute trades.

## Five Whys — root cause
1. Investors struggle to find relevant reliable information because they must review lots of content.
2. Content is spread across separate news, markets, company and social-media services.
3. Users manually search, compare and filter.
4. News volume, relevance and trustworthiness change rapidly.
5. They lack a central personalized method to consolidate and prioritize information.

**Root cause:** Fragmented information sources without a unified filtering/analysis process.

## Fishbone — five categories
- **People:** Limited research time, uneven experience, difficulty judging credibility.
- **Process:** Repetitive manual searches and comparisons, inconsistent filtering.
- **Machines:** Separate nonintegrated platforms and changing feeds.
- **Materials:** News, social posts, company reports and price data.
- **Measurement:** Hard to score source trust, rank relevance or separate trends from noise.

Both Jake's and Henry's analyses use these five categories. Source files include visual fishbone diagrams.

## Sprint 2 feasibility and options
1. Status quo — users keep multiple tools.
2. Simple aggregator — easier but still leaves manual evaluation.
3. Focused FinSight MVP — watchlists, filters, source attribution, summaries, alerts and limited trends.

The preliminary report recommends **advancing to analysis of the focused MVP**, rather than approving every future feature. Technical feasibility depends on API integration and AI accuracy. Economic feasibility requires real licensing and cloud costs. Operational feasibility depends on usability and trust. Schedule feasibility requires strict MVP scope.
